# Ondera 0.10.0

## Tempo changes inside a song
- A song can change tempo anywhere after its start, at once or by a ramp (a ritardando or an
  accelerando that glides from the tempo before, linear in beats).
- The **tempo track** (View > Tempo Track, `⇧T`) sits under the ruler: click to add a change,
  drag a point to move it or change its tempo (`⇧` for tenths of a BPM, `⌥` off the grid),
  double-click to type one, right-click to make it ramp or delete it.
- The transport display shows the tempo at the playhead, and dragging or typing it edits the
  tempo in force there. The SMPTE clock follows the changes.
- Notes, regions, markers and automation keep their bars and beats: only the speed changes.
  Audio regions keep their bars too and play their audio at its own speed through every change,
  their fades included.
- Playback, the metronome, the count-in, exports, bounces, stems and plugins' transport follow
  the tempo map; audio regions placed from the browser or recorded cover the bars their seconds
  take at that point of the song.
- MIDI files carry tempo changes both ways: export writes every change (a ramp as a step each
  sixteenth note, each as long as that stretch of the ramp) and import with the file's tempo
  follows the file's changes.
- New commands: `tempo.list`, `tempo.set`, `tempo.move`, `tempo.remove`, `tempo.clear`;
  `transport.locate` reports the tempo and the time in seconds at the playhead;
  `session.overview` lists the changes.
- Songs that keep one tempo read and write exactly as before.

## Buses: groups and aux returns
- **Track > Add bus** makes a bus track. It holds no regions and sums what reaches it through
  its own inserts, fader, pan, mute and solo, then goes to the Stereo Out.
- **Groups**: the inspector's **Output** menu routes a track to a bus; right-click a track and
  choose *Route Track to a New Bus* to make one in a click.
- **Aux returns**: click a send's name to point it at a bus, or add one with **+ Send**. A track
  has up to four sends; the first two feed A · Reverb and B · Delay until pointed elsewhere.
- Buses feed the Stereo Out, A and B, never another bus, so the mix cannot loop. Removing a bus
  sends its tracks back to the Stereo Out, in one undo step with the removal.
- Solo on a bus keeps the tracks that feed it audible; solo on a track keeps its bus.
- Plugin delay compensation works in stages (tracks, buses, A and B, master), so a latent plugin
  on a bus stays on the same sample as everything else.
- Stems follow the routing: a track's stem goes through its bus when effects are included, and
  a bus's stem is everything routed to it.
- New commands: `track.add kind=bus`, `track.setOutput`, `strip.setSend`, `track.group`;
  `track.list`, `strip.get` and `session.overview` show outputs, inputs and send targets, and
  the overview flags a track whose bus is muted.

## Plugins and MIDI
- `strip.programs` reports the Audio Unit factory preset in use, including after the song is
  reopened.
- Reading an Audio Unit's factory presets no longer leaks the list each time (Apple's
  documentation makes the host its owner); loading one keeps the preset's name alive while it is
  set. Tried on Apple's units, Serum 2, Auto-Tune Access and Omnivox.
- The browser's row menu loads a plugin installed in several formats as any of them (*Load as
  VST3*, *Insert as Audio Unit*); the row itself still loads CLAP, then VST3, then AU.
- `session.importMidi keepChannels=true` keeps one track per track of the file, every note and
  controller on its own channel, for a multitimbral instrument.
- Polyphonic pressure is sent again to notes a locate starts, keys without a note let go of it,
  and stop returns every pressed key to zero. It reaches the instrument after the note it
  presses on.

## Known limits
- Time signature changes inside a song, time stretching and comping are not built yet. Buses do
  not feed other buses.
- The tempo track, buses and the new plugin commands were checked in the real window and by
  tests; playback through speakers, a real interface and a MIDI keyboard was not tried.
- Monitoring and the count-in have still not been tried with an audio interface.
